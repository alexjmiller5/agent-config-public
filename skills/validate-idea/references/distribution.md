# Distribution and offer checklist

Use when selecting or criticizing an idea's acquisition path. Scan every row;
report the strongest fit plus the strongest rejected alternatives, not every
possible tactic. Give each candidate **test now / later / poor fit / unknown**
with an idea-specific reason. Choose one primary channel and one bounded test
that fit founder time, cash, access and time to revenue. Verify current platform
rules, availability, fees and audience before relying on them.

| Channel | Applicable when | Cheapest useful test | Core metric | Main pitfall |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Founder-led B2B outreach | Identifiable budget owners have an urgent expensive job; founder can reach them | One narrow prospect list, warm introductions and a manual paid pilot proposal | Qualified conversations -> paid pilots -> retained accounts; hours and cash per win | Directory entries are not consent; procurement, customization and sales time can erase margin |
| Partners and affiliates | A trusted adviser, supplier or publisher already reaches the exact buyer | Discuss one specific referral offer with one relevant partner | Attributed retained customers and contribution after commission | Channel conflict, low-quality leads and unclear attribution; do not build a program before a referral works |
| Niche creators, revenue share and UGC | Audience buys the outcome, product demos well, creator fit and trust are credible | Find creators directly or via a marketplace; shortlist by audience fit and propose one original demo with a simple repeatable brief and tracked offer | Qualified visits -> activation -> retained payers; contribution after creator cost | Views do not prove demand; agree attribution window, payout basis, refunds, usage rights and disclosure; synthetic content must not fake customer experience |
| Paid search | Buyers actively search for a defined solution with purchase intent | Inspect intent and competition, then propose a capped ad-to-offer test | Paid conversion, contribution-adjusted acquisition cost and payback | Keyword volume is not buyer intent; click cost without conversion/retention makes a cheap lead expensive |
| Paid social and subreddit ads | A recognizable problem can be demonstrated to an available target audience | Compare a few original creative angles with fixed spend and a stop rule | Retained payer acquisition cost, not reach or click-through alone | Creative fatigue, weak targeting and tracking; one viral ad does not establish repeatability |
| Long-tail SEO and educational content | Specific recurring searches match the buyer's job and useful original content can compete | One high-intent page, worked example or relevant video answering a real query | Qualified organic traffic -> activated users -> paying customers | Narrow keywords do not guarantee ranking; publishing, indexing and authority take time; faceless video is still a production workload |
| App-store discovery and localization | Product is store-shaped; a language/region has evidence of buyer demand and viable payments/support | Compare local competitors and search intent; test accurate listing/demo messages with target-language users | Listing -> install -> activation -> paid renewal, by locale | Keyword stuffing and literal translation are not positioning; language, cultural fit, store terms and support all matter |
| Communities and Reddit | Buyers discuss the job in an accessible community whose rules permit participation | Read complaints and comments, then propose one useful answer, demo or honest waitlist test if allowed | Qualified conversations -> meaningful commitments; track source | Founder communities may contain peers rather than buyers; no spam, fake endorsements or promotion disguised as research; comments can convert but require attribution |
| Launch platforms and deal marketplaces | Product/audience matches Product Hunt, Hacker News or AppSumo buyers | Verify fit and terms; propose one focused launch, not a launch tour | Qualified activation and retained buyers; net contribution after fees | Launch-day votes are not retention; lifetime deals create ongoing service liabilities and do not prove recurring revenue |
| Build in public and owned audience | Actual buyers follow the subject and founder wants a sustainable public presence | One buyer-focused example or feature outcome with a measurable next action | Buyer replies, qualified leads and later paid conversion | Developer followers may never buy; privacy, attention cost and founder preference matter; audience building is not free |
| Referrals and word of mouth | Users already get repeat value and have a natural reason to share with similar buyers | One optional referral offer to engaged users, capped and manually checked | Incremental activated/retained payers per referrer; reward-adjusted contribution | Verified email is not activation; fraud, self-referrals and rewarding nonbuyers can consume margin; product quality does not guarantee sharing |
| Adjacent free tools, templates and tentpole content | A small useful asset serves the same buyer and leads naturally to the paid job | One template, calculator, sample or mini-tool with a clear next step | Qualified asset users -> paid product use; maintenance per acquired customer | A network of unrelated mini-apps is a second business, not automatic SEO multiplication; avoid building before testing a simple sample |

## Offer and experiment design

Sell the customer's improved outcome, not the machinery. A landing page, demo,
listing, checkout and follow-up should carry the same narrow promise, price and
reason to trust it. Make the next action easy and measure drop-off; a fixed click
count or clever domain does not establish demand. Offer truthful availability and
clear terms. A waitlist tests interest, a paid pilot tests purchase, and a renewal
tests recurring value. Record denominators separately at each step.

Study competitor messages, review complaints, search queries, creative formats
and feature requests to generate hypotheses. Use original execution and assets
or obtain appropriate rights. A feature request is not a purchase order; trend
attention is not durable demand. Treat review authenticity and third-party
traffic/revenue estimates as uncertain unless independently verified.

Good hooks, creator reach and effective selling can acquire customers for an
ordinary feature-parity product. Test that commercial route without demanding
technical novelty; views establish interest, while paid conversion and retention
establish different parts of viability.

For organic or creator content, test hooks tied to a real customer need; relevant
original demonstrations beat borrowed popularity as evidence. Neither famous
clips, AI UGC, a particular social network, a gender/industry niche nor an early
trend guarantees views, conversion or retention. Measure a small set of angles,
repeat a winner only while conversion and contribution hold, and watch fatigue.
Marketing explains value honestly; misleading claims, copied assets and hidden
endorsements are not distribution advantages.

## From first sale to a repeatable business

- Anchor a pricing test to competitor offers, full delivery cost and demonstrated
  customer value. Choose the charging unit to match usage: per outcome, seat,
  team, location or consumption. A subscription needs repeated value; episodic
  work may fit a one-off purchase. Free limits or game unlocks should expose the
  value honestly and be tested for paid conversion and retained play.
- Include creator/affiliate shares, store fees, rewards, refunds, content rights,
  localization and support in the relevant contribution or acquisition cost.
  Count an expense once; distinguish ongoing revenue share from upfront CAC.
  Backsolve the revenue or profit goal into retained customers and workload;
  MRR is neither cash collected nor profit.
- Land one team/use-case with a small sale, then test adjacent paid seats,
  usage or teams through a real internal champion and budget owner. Expansion
  is a hypothesis, not automatic internal selling or a cure for poor retention.
- For a recurring cohort, NRR = (starting recurring revenue + expansion -
  contraction - churn) / starting recurring revenue. Exclude new customers;
  use the same period and revenue basis. Above 100% means that cohort's revenue
  grew, not that every customer stayed or the business is profitable. Track
  customer retention and contribution alongside it; NRR is undefined when
  starting recurring revenue is zero.
- Prove one acquisition -> paid outcome -> repeat-value loop before multiplying
  products or channels. Choose the next move from measured bottlenecks, not a
  universal revenue-stage rule for ads versus influencers. Preserve learning
  from failed tests and change one hypothesis before repeating them.
