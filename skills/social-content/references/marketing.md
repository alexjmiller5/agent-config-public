# Marketing judgment for social content

Start with who needs this and why they would care now. The reader's task
comes before the product name. Use one primary objective for a post, such
as explaining a technique, inviting informed feedback, or reaching people
who need a demonstrated capability.

## Form a compact brief

Capture audience/problem, offer or idea, supporting evidence, distinctive
tradeoff, author relationship, desired next step, and voice. Reuse supplied
product context. Ask for a missing fact only when it changes the choice of
angle or makes a claim unsafe to write.

Distinguish these levels of proof:

| Available material | What it supports |
| --- | --- |
| Implemented feature | A statement of that capability |
| Demonstration | What happened under the demonstrated conditions |
| Measured experiment | Its result with the sample, comparison, and limits |
| Customer statement | That person's attributed experience, with permission |

A feature's intended benefit is not a measured outcome. "Designed to reduce
manual checking" and "halved checking time" require different evidence.

## Make the post useful

Offer an explanation, reproducible method, revealing example, candid
tradeoff, useful artifact, or specific answer. The reader should get value
even if they never visit the product. For commercial content, disclose the
author's connection naturally and keep the offer proportionate to the
destination. Do not disguise an advertisement as an independent discovery.

Match the next step to intent. Someone learning a method may need the
instructions in the post; someone evaluating a tool may need a permitted
demo link. A discussion does not need to become a lead funnel. Avoid fake
scarcity, hidden affiliation, and invented testimonials.

## Voice and repetition

Use the author's actual language and point of view. Remove padded claims,
stock enthusiasm, and generic calls for agreement. Do not add typos or false
anecdotes to disguise AI assistance. Show enough detail to make the claim
credible without burying the point under caveats.

When planning multiple posts, cover different audience questions and stages
of understanding. Reuse evidence and adapt the argument to each destination.
Choose a sustainable cadence from audience response and community rules;
do not impose a universal posting quota or repeat a pitch across communities.
